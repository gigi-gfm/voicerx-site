Beat the Heat

When the body can’t cool itself, trouble builds fast. Knowing the two stages — and acting early — can prevent an emergency.

Heat Exhaustion

  • Heavy sweating; cool, clammy, pale skin
  • Dizziness, headache, nausea
  • Weak, fast pulse; muscle cramps
What to do

Move to shade/AC, sip water, loosen clothing, apply cool wet cloths. Rest until fully recovered.

Heat Stroke

Emergency
  • Temp 103°F+; hot, red, dry or damp skin
  • Confusion, slurred speech, fainting
  • Strong, racing pulse
⚠ Call 911 immediately

Move to cool, cool aggressively (wet skin, fan, ice packs to neck/armpits). Don’t give fluids if not fully alert.

#1
Top weather killer

Heat causes more U.S. deaths most years than floods, tornadoes or hurricanes.

10min
Never leave anyone in a car

Interiors can climb ~20°F in 10 minutes — deadly for kids & pets.

★
Higher-risk

Infants, older adults, athletes & some meds raise heat risk. Check on neighbors.

Hydration & Sun Safety

Drink Before You’re Thirsty

Thirst lags behind your body’s needs, so sip steadily all day — don’t wait for the dry mouth.

Stay ahead of it
  • Carry water; add fruit or a pinch of electrolytes on sweaty days
  • Limit sugary & alcoholic drinks — they dry you out
  • Pee-check: pale yellow = good; dark = drink up
  • “Eat your water”: watermelon, cucumber & berries are ~90% water

Smart Sun Protection

SPF 30+, broad-spectrumReapply every 2 hours & after swimming or sweating.
Seek shade 10am–4pmUV is strongest midday — hats & sunglasses help too.
Don’t forget spotsEars, nose, neck, scalp part & tops of feet burn easily.
Burn? Cool & moisturizeCool bath, aloe, fluids; see us for blistering burns.
15min
Sunburn starts fast

Unprotected skin can burn in as little as 15 minutes of midday sun.

1.5L
Sweat adds up

You can lose over a liter of fluid an hour in the heat — replace it.

Heads-up: some prescriptions increase sun & heat sensitivity. Ask Dr. Tess whether any of yours do.

Bites, Stings & Bugs

Bee & Wasp Stings

Scrape the stinger out sideways — don’t pinch (that pushes in more venom). Wash, then cold compress.

  • Ice 10 min on/off for swelling
  • Antihistamine for itch, ibuprofen for pain
⚠ Call 911 if

Lips/tongue/throat swell, hives spread, or breathing is hard — use an EpiPen if prescribed.

Mosquitoes & Ticks

Prevent
  • EPA-approved repellent; cover skin at dusk
  • Dump standing water around the yard
  • Check for ticks after trails & tall grass
Tick found?

Pull straight out with tweezers at the skin. Watch for a spreading rash, fever or aches — and tell us.

Know the Warning Signs of a Serious Reaction

Trouble breathing or swallowing
Swelling of lips, tongue or throat
Dizziness or fainting
Hives spreading beyond the sting

Summer Skin

Heat, sweat, sun and plants all test your skin in summer. Most flare-ups are minor and settle quickly with simple care.

Heat Rash

Trapped sweat plugs the skin’s ducts, leaving tiny red, prickly bumps — often in folds, on the neck or chest.

Soothe & prevent

Cool down, keep skin dry, wear loose cotton, skip heavy creams. Usually clears in a few days.

Sunburn Relief

  • Cool bath/compress; aloe or fragrance-free lotion
  • Extra fluids; ibuprofen for pain
  • Leave blisters intact; don’t peel
⚠ See a doctor for

Widespread blistering, fever, chills or signs of dehydration.

Poison Ivy, Oak & Sumac

Leaves of 3

“Leaves of three, let it be.” The oil (urushiol) causes an itchy, blistering rash 12–72 hours after contact.

Right away

Rinse skin with soap & cool water within the hour; wash clothes, tools & pets that touched it.

⚠ Get care if

Rash hits the face/eyes/groin, covers large areas, or oozes & spreads — we can help calm it.

Get Moving

12 Smart Summer Activities

Swimming

Cool, low-impact, great for all ages. Swim with a buddy; watch kids closely.

Family Bike Rides

Explore the trails together. Helmets on; go early to beat the heat.

Nature Walks & Hikes

Free, easy, good for the heart. Water, sunscreen, bug spray; check for ticks.

Gardening

Gentle strength & stress relief. Hat, gloves, shade breaks, hydrate.

Backyard Sports

Catch, soccer, frisbee. Warm up; take water & shade timeouts.

Farmers Markets

Walk & shop fresh produce. Morning trips dodge midday heat.

Sunrise/Sunset Strolls

Beat the heat at the cool ends of day. Reflective gear after dark.

Picnics in the Park

Family time outdoors. Keep cold food cold; shade the spread.

Sprinkler & Water Play

Kid-favorite way to cool off. Non-slip surfaces; supervise always.

Yoga / Stretch on the Lawn

Calm, flexible, joint-friendly. Early shade; mat & water.

Splash Pads & Pools

Great for little ones. Reapply sunscreen; rest in shade.

Evening Ball Games

Community fun & cardio. Hydrate; bug spray at dusk.

Water, Travel & Cookout Safety

Water Safety

  • Never swim alone; actively watch kids — drowning is silent
  • Life jackets on boats & open water (not floaties)
  • Enter feet-first in unknown water; know the depth
  • Learn the signs of “dry/quiet” drowning after a scare

Cookout Food Safety

  • Keep cold food cold; don’t leave it out over 2 hrs (1 hr if 90°F+)
  • Separate raw & cooked; use a meat thermometer
  • Wash hands & surfaces; clean the cooler

Road-Trip Ready

Water & snacks for everyoneStretch & hydrate every couple of hours.
Pack a basic first-aid kitBandages, antihistamine, pain reliever, sunscreen.
Bring your meds & a listPlus GFM’s number saved in your phone.
Never leave kids/pets in the carNot even for a quick errand.

Did You Know?

Summer Health Factoids

A handful of surprising-but-true facts to share around the cooler.

🐝
Bees sting once; wasps don’t

Honeybees die after stinging — wasps & hornets can sting again and again. Back away calmly, don’t swat.

🌡️
Humidity makes heat worse

In muggy air, sweat can’t evaporate — so your body’s main cooling system stalls.

🍉
You can eat your water

Watermelon, cucumber & strawberries are about 90% water — tasty hydration.

🐾
Hooch’s tip

Dogs cool by panting, not sweating. Shade, water & cool ground keep our Comfort Specialist happy.

☀️
UV sneaks through clouds

Up to 80% of UV passes through clouds — you can burn on an overcast day.

💧
Thirst is a late signal

By the time you feel thirsty you’re already mildly dehydrated — sip ahead of it.

🚗
Cars heat up shockingly fast

On an 80°F day a car can pass 100°F in minutes — never leave kids or pets.

🦟
Still water = mosquitoes

They breed in tiny amounts of standing water. Empty saucers, buckets & gutters weekly.
