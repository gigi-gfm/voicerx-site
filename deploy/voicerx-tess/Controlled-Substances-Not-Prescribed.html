Controlled Substances We Do Not Prescribe

For patient safety and scope of practice, Dr. Tess does not prescribe, refill, or manage the controlled substances below — including new prescriptions, refills, early refills, and one-time "bridge" fills. We are glad to coordinate a referral to the right specialist.

Organized by DEA schedule, from most tightly controlled (Schedule II) to least (Schedule V). Brand names are examples only — the policy covers the generic medication and all brand/combination forms. This list is not exhaustive; when in doubt, assume a controlled substance is not prescribed here and ask the front desk.

  • Morphine (MS Contin)

  • Oxymorphone (Opana)

  • Codeine (single-ingredient)

  • Amphetamine salts (Adderall)

  • Ketamine (incl. compounded)

A note on gabapentin (Neurontin): not federally scheduled, but controlled in several states and managed cautiously here — generally not started or refilled without a clear, coordinated plan.

Why we have this policy

  • These medications are safest with one consistent prescriber.
  • State/DEA rules require continuity, agreements, and monitoring (PDMP).
  • Prevents duplicate or conflicting prescriptions.

What we can do

  • Treat your other medical needs as usual.
  • Refer to pain management, psychiatry, sleep, or addiction medicine.
  • Send records to your controlled-med prescriber.
  • Offer non-controlled alternatives where appropriate.

This is a practice policy, not medical advice. Never stop a controlled medication abruptly — talk with the prescriber who started it.
